Editorial methodology

How the catalog classifies a medium that never stands still.

The catalog preserves the supplied inventory’s terminology and approximate popularity periods, then adds a transparent navigation layer for eras, families, relationships, search, and teaching.

1. Source-derived fields

Every launch entry begins with the title, main popularity period, common names, description, and typical elements in the supplied Website Design Trends History document. Those fields remain distinct from editorial additions so that readers can tell which information comes from the project inventory.

2. Popularity periods are approximate

The source explicitly warns that web-design movements rarely begin or end cleanly. They overlap, reappear as nostalgia, and continue in particular sectors. The date labels therefore indicate a main period of popularity, not a claim about the first or last possible example.

3. Umbrella families support navigation

The eleven family pages use the source’s broader groupings: Early-Web Vernacular, Immersive Multimedia, Realistic or Tactile UI, Reductionist UI, Modular UI, Editorial Design, Retro-Futurism, Expressive Anti-Minimalism, Atmospheric Surfaces, Adaptive Architecture, and Interaction-Led Design.

A trend can appear in more than one family because the same movement may be simultaneously editorial, interactive, atmospheric, or architectural. A primary family is selected for card labeling; secondary families remain available in filters and entry metadata.

4. Era pages are discovery aids

Decade pages group entries by dates named in their popularity period. They should not be read as rigid periodization. A movement listed in the 2010s may remain influential in the 2020s, and a 1990s visual language may return as a deliberate revival.

5. Relationships are editorial links

Related and contrasting entries are added to support learning. These links help readers move between close terms, predecessors, reactions, and revivals, but they are not asserted as a complete scholarly genealogy.

6. Images carry evidence labels

Original catalog illustrations are labeled Representative reconstruction. They isolate characteristic layouts, colors, typography, and interface cues without claiming to be an archival capture. User-supplied or archival images are labeled separately and require clear source and rights metadata before publication.

7. Corrections remain visible

Substantive changes to titles, dates, descriptions, source notes, or image credits should be recorded in the public changelog. Contributions are evaluated against the supplied inventory and any supporting primary or archival evidence.

Interpretation rule: A date range describes when a style was broadly visible, not when every website adopted or abandoned it.
