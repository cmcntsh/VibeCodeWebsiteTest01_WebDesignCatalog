About the project

A field guide for reading the web’s visual history.

Web Design Trend Catalog helps beginners, educators, researchers, and working designers identify a style, learn its common names, place it in time, and follow its relationships to other movements.

Catalog scope
64 launch entries
Historical range
1991 to the present
Core modes
Search, timeline, styles, comparison

Why this catalog exists

People often recognize a visual language before they know what to call it. They may remember “shiny blue buttons,” “a 1990s personal homepage,” or “the style with thick black borders,” but lack the terminology needed to research it. The catalog connects those visual memories to names, periods, descriptions, and recurring elements.

It is designed for the vibe-code era, when more people can build interfaces quickly but may not yet have a structured vocabulary for evaluating what those interfaces resemble or where their conventions came from.

What it is—and is not

This is a navigable educational reference, not a ranking of good and bad aesthetics. Earlier designs are treated in relation to their tools, browser conditions, bandwidth, publishing culture, and visual ambitions. Popularity periods are presented as approximate ranges because movements overlap, survive in specific sectors, and return through nostalgia.

The catalog also distinguishes original representative reconstructions from archival captures. A reconstruction demonstrates recognizable traits; it does not prove that a particular historical website looked exactly that way.

The editorial promise

  • Use plain language without discarding accurate terminology.
  • Preserve uncertainty where dates or labels do not have clean boundaries.
  • Identify editorial additions instead of presenting them as source material.
  • Credit images and sources clearly.
  • Correct errors transparently through the changelog.
